# Chef Knife Care Guide

*Owner: Merchandising & Customer Education · Last reviewed: 2026-10-14*

A good chef knife can last decades with a few simple habits. This guide covers everyday care for knives sold in the Home & Kitchen category at ShopFlow.

## Before You Use It

- Wash the knife by hand with warm water and mild dish soap before first use.
- Dry it right away with a soft towel.
- Keep the original packaging until you're sure you're keeping the knife. Returns require items to be unused and in their original packaging (see [Returns](#returns-and-defects)).

## Everyday Cleaning

1. **Hand wash only.** Dishwashers expose blades to heat, harsh detergent, and knocking against other items. This dulls the edge and can damage handles.
2. **Wash right after use**, especially after cutting acidic foods such as tomatoes, citrus, or onions.
3. **Wipe from spine to edge**, with the blade facing away from you.
4. **Dry completely** before storing. Even stainless steel can spot or rust if left damp.

Don't soak knives. Prolonged water exposure can loosen handles and cause staining.

## Cutting Surfaces

- Use wood or soft plastic cutting boards.
- Avoid glass, ceramic, marble, and granite surfaces. They dull the edge quickly.
- Don't use the knife to cut through bones, frozen food, or hard shells. Use a cleaver or the right tool for the job.
- Scrape food off with the spine of the blade, not the edge.

## Storage

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Magnetic strip | Place the spine on the strip first, then lay the blade down. Keep the edge from scraping the magnet. |
| Knife block | Insert the blade with the spine against the slot's top edge. Make sure the block is dry inside. |
| Blade guard or sheath | Good for drawers. Make sure the knife is fully dry before sheathing. |

Never leave knives loose in a drawer. The edge will chip and it's a safety hazard.

## Keeping the Edge Sharp

- **Honing** straightens the edge. Hone lightly before or after every few uses with a honing rod.
- **Sharpening** removes a small amount of steel to restore the edge. Most home cooks only need to sharpen once or twice a year, using a whetstone or a quality sharpening system.
- A dull knife is more dangerous than a sharp one because it needs more force and slips more easily.

## Handle Care

- **Wood handles:** Occasionally rub with food-safe mineral oil to prevent drying and cracking.
- **Composite or plastic handles:** Wipe clean and dry. No special treatment is needed.
- Avoid leaving knives in direct sunlight or near the stove, where heat can warp or discolor handles.

## Removing Stains and Rust Spots

For light discoloration or small rust spots on stainless steel, make a paste of baking soda and water. Rub gently along the blade with a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge, then rinse and dry well. Don't use steel wool or abrasive scouring pads. If rust keeps returning shortly after delivery, it may be a product issue (see below).
