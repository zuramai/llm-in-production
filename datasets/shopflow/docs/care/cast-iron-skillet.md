# Cast Iron Skillet Care Guide

*Owner: Customer Care Content · Last reviewed: 2026-10-14*

A cast iron skillet can last for generations if you look after it. This guide covers first use, everyday cleaning, re-seasoning, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Before First Use

1. Wash the skillet with warm water and a soft brush or sponge. A small amount of mild soap is fine at this stage.
2. Dry it completely with a towel, then heat it on the stove for 2-3 minutes to remove any remaining moisture.
3. Rub a very thin layer of high smoke point oil (such as vegetable, canola, or flaxseed) over the whole skillet, inside and out, including the handle.
4. Wipe off the excess until the surface looks almost dry. Too much oil leads to a sticky finish.

## Seasoning in the Oven

Seasoning is baked-on oil that creates a natural nonstick surface. To build or refresh it:

1. Heat the oven to 450°F (230°C).
2. Place the oiled skillet **upside down** on the middle rack, with a sheet of foil on the rack below to catch drips.
3. Bake for 1 hour, then turn the oven off and let the skillet cool inside.
4. Repeat 2-3 times for a stronger base layer.

Light smoke from the oil during seasoning is normal. Open a window or run your vent fan.

## Everyday Cleaning

- **Clean while still warm.** Use hot water and a stiff brush or non-metal scrubber.
- **For stuck-on food,** simmer a little water in the pan for a few minutes, then scrape with a wooden or silicone spatula. Coarse salt also works as a gentle scrub.
- **Dry right away,** either by hand or on a low burner for a minute.
- **Add a few drops of oil** and wipe the surface with a paper towel until it has a light sheen.

## What to Avoid

- **Dishwashers.** They strip the seasoning and cause rust.
- **Soaking.** Never leave the skillet in water.
- **Sudden temperature changes.** Don't run cold water over a very hot pan, because this can cause warping or cracking.
- **Long cooking of acidic foods** such as tomato sauce, wine reductions, or citrus, especially while the seasoning is young.
- **Steel wool or harsh abrasives,** except when restoring rust (see below).

## Fixing Rust or Sticky Spots

**Rust:** Scrub the affected area with steel wool and warm water until the rust is gone. Rinse, dry thoroughly, and re-season in the oven as described above. A rusty skillet is not ruined.

**Sticky or tacky surface:** This usually means the oil layer was too thick. Bake the skillet upside down at 450°F for 1 hour to harden it.

**Flaking or dull seasoning:** Scrub lightly, dry, and add another seasoning round or two.

## Storage

Store the skillet in a dry place. If you stack cookware, put a paper towel or pan protector inside the skillet to absorb moisture and prevent scratches. Avoid storing it with a lid on when it's damp.

## Handling Safety

Cast iron holds heat for a long time. The handle gets very hot on the stove and in the oven, so always use an oven mitt or a handle cover. Lift with both hands when the skillet is full.
