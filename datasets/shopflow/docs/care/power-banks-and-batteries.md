# Power Banks and Battery Devices: Care and Safety Guide

*Owner: Customer Care Content · Last reviewed: 2026-10-14*

This guide explains how to use, store, and charge power banks and other battery-powered devices bought from ShopFlow (Electronics, Sports & Outdoors, Toys & Games, and Office). It also covers what to do if something goes wrong.

## Stop using the product immediately if you notice

- **Swelling:** the case bulges, gaps appear along the seams, or the device no longer sits flat.
- **Overheating:** the device is too hot to hold comfortably, during charging or in normal use.
- **Smoke, a burning smell, or a hissing sound.**
- **Leaking fluid** or discoloration on the casing.

If any of these happen:

1. Unplug the device and stop using it. Do not charge it again.
2. If it is safe to do so, move it onto a non-flammable surface (tile, concrete, metal) away from furniture, bedding, and paper.
3. Do not press on, puncture, or try to open a swollen battery.
4. If there is smoke or fire, leave the area and call 911.
5. Once it is safe, [contact support](mailto:support@shopflow.example) with a photo, your order number, and the SKU (for example, SF-ELE-0073).

Safety reports are treated as **urgent** tickets and handled first (first response target: 1 hour during support hours). Please do not wait to see whether the problem goes away.

## Everyday care

- **Use the right charger.** Use the cable and adapter supplied, or ones that meet the device's rated voltage and current.
- **Don't charge on soft surfaces.** Beds, sofas, and pillows trap heat.
- **Avoid extreme temperatures.** Don't leave devices in a parked car, in direct sun, or near heaters. Keep them between roughly 32°F and 95°F (0°C and 35°C) when charging.
- **Don't use damaged cables or cases.** Replace frayed cables. Don't use a device that has been dropped hard or crushed until you've checked it for swelling or dents.
- **Avoid unattended overnight charging** where possible.
- **Keep away from water.** Unless the product is rated as water resistant, keep it dry.

## Storage and travel

- For long-term storage, keep the battery around 40-60% charged in a cool, dry place.
- Recharge stored devices every few months.
- Carry power banks in your cabin bag when flying, not in checked luggage. Check the airline's rules on capacity limits.
- Keep terminals from touching metal objects such as keys or coins.

## Disposal

Never throw lithium-ion batteries or power banks in household trash or curbside recycling, as they can start fires in collection trucks and facilities.

- Take them to a household hazardous waste site or a battery drop-off point at a local recycling center.
- Tape over exposed terminals, or place each device in its own plastic bag.
- A damaged or swollen battery should be stored in a non-flammable container, such as a metal tin with sand, until you can drop it off. Tell the facility it is damaged.
