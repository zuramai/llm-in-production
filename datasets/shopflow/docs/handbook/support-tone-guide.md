# Support Tone Guide

*Owner: Support Enablement · Last reviewed: 2026-10-14*

## Purpose

This guide covers how we write to customers in chat, email, macros, and any AI-drafted replies. Our tone is warm, plain, and specific. Our promises are limited to what policy allows.

## Why we never promise outside policy

In 2024, a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada responsible for wrong refund information its website chatbot gave a customer. The airline argued the chatbot was a separate source, and the tribunal rejected that. The lesson applies to us: **anything a ShopFlow agent, macro, or bot tells a customer is something ShopFlow said.** A kind but incorrect promise can become an obligation, so when you are unsure, say what you will check instead of guessing.

## Tone principles

- **Be human and brief.** Lead with the answer, then the next step.
- **Own the problem, not the blame.** Apologize once, sincerely, and move on.
- **Be specific.** Use real timeframes and amounts from policy, not "soon."
- **Say no kindly.** Explain the reason, then offer what we can do.
- **Avoid hedging words** like "definitely," "guaranteed," or "I'll make sure," unless policy backs them.

## Good vs. bad examples

**Refund timing**
- ❌ "Your refund will be in your account tomorrow!"
- ✅ "Once we receive and inspect your return, the refund goes to your original payment method within 5–7 business days."

**Refund to a different card**
- ❌ "Sure, I can send it to your new card."
- ✅ "I understand the old card is inconvenient. For security, we can only refund to the original payment method. Your bank can tell you how they handle refunds to replaced cards."

**Opened beauty item**
- ❌ "No worries, just send it back, we'll sort it out."
- ✅ "Opened beauty products can't be returned unless they arrived damaged or defective. If yours was damaged, send me a photo and I'll look into it."

**Package marked delivered**
- ❌ "We'll reship it today."
- ✅ "Carriers sometimes mark packages delivered early, so please check with neighbors and your mail room and wait 24 hours. If it hasn't arrived, reply within 14 days of the delivery date and we'll open a carrier investigation."

**Damaged item**
- ❌ "That's so frustrating, we'll refund you whatever you want."
- ✅ "I'm sorry it arrived damaged. Please send a photo, and we'll offer a free replacement or a full refund including shipping."

**Delivery estimates**
- ❌ "It'll be there Thursday for sure."
- ✅ "Standard US shipping takes 3–5 business days. The tracking link will show the carrier's latest estimate."

## Safety issues

If a customer mentions overheating, smoke, or a swelling battery, drop the template. Tell them to **stop using the product immediately**, set the ticket to **urgent** (1-hour first response), and escalate. Do not ask them to test it again.

## Refunds and approvals

- Never tell a customer a refund is approved until it is. Agents approve up to $100, team leads up to $500, and anything above $500 goes to Finance via the finance-approvals channel.
- Say: "I'm requesting this now. Some refunds need an additional review, and I'll update you by email."
- Refunds are idempotent per order and reason. Don't promise "an extra refund" for the same issue.

## Things we never offer

- Crypto, cash on delivery, or gift cards (we don't sell them)
- Refunds to a different card or account
- Address changes after an order has shipped
- Exceptions to the 30-day return window without lead approval

## Quick checklist before sending

1. Is every date, amount, and timeframe in the policy or FAQ?
2. Did I promise an outcome, or describe a process?
3. Did I give the customer one clear next step?
4. Would I be comfortable if this message were quoted in a dispute?

If any answer is no, rewrite it or ask a team lead. For account data questions, never paste customer emails into AI tools. Those tools use a read-only role that cannot see them.
